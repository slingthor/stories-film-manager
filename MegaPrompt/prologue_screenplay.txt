OPENING SEQUENCE - THE DYING WITNESS

SHOT -1B: THE RAVEN'S DYING THROAT
INT. RAVEN'S THROAT - DAY/NIGHT/ALL TIMES

FADE IN:

Complete darkness. Then, a pinprick of light far ahead.

We are INSIDE something organic. Pink tissue walls pulse weakly around us—dying cartilage, weathered from a thousand years of testimony.

The throat passage is filmed with endoscopic brutality. Wet, clinical, anatomical. This was Odin's raven—Muninn (Memory)—dying on black sand after accumulating too much witness to fly.

Three labored breaths illuminate fragments deeper in the tunnel:
- A whale's eye reflecting golden slaughter
- Families counting themselves at abundant tables
- The eternal repetition of prosperity before extraction

On the third breath, the passage opens fully. We emerge through the beak into—

SHOT -1: THE SHADOW POLE VIGIL
EXT. HEADLAND ABOVE WESTFJORDS - GOLDEN HOUR (JUNE 1887)

—impossible summer.

The Westfjords dressed in false prosperity. Grass so green it seems painted. Purple-black krækilyng berries dripping juice without being touched.

On the headland: a POLE. Eight feet of grey driftwood, wrapped at the top with rope whose origin predates memory. The pole casts a shadow 200 feet long—geometrically impossible for its height, for the sun's angle, for physics itself.

SIGRID SIGURÐSDÓTTIR (16) stands near the pole. Wheat-blonde hair braided in the unmarried style. White linen dress that her dead mother wove.

She opens her mouth to sing. But the voice that emerges is MALE, elderly, a bass tone from her young throat:

SIGRID/MALE VOICE
(in Icelandic, subtitled)
Krummi svaf í klettagjá,
kaldri vetrarnóttu á...

Behind her, the ocean RISES three feet. Holds for two seconds. DROPS three feet.

The sea is breathing.

SHOT 0B: THE FALSE FEAST PREPARATION
EXT. FIELD ABOVE BEACH - DAY (ESTABLISHING)

WIDE SHOT revealing scale of false prosperity:

Fifty people constructing feast tables from driftwood—Norwegian pine from settlement ships, Danish oak from trade vessels, British steel bolts from vessels not yet wrecked.

A WHALE—70 feet of fin whale—lies on the beach being flensed. Steam rises from the opening in its body. But visible in its spine: BRASS FIXTURES, industrial bolts grown INTO the bone, not added but part of its skeleton.

Women collecting blood in buckets before iron seeps into sand. But the blood has ribbons of gold through it—divine ichor mixed with mortal fluid.

Children play, making patterns with sheep knucklebones. Every throw creates the same pattern: five points with six intersections. Impossible. They don't notice.

MAGNÚS ÞORLÁKSSON (45, not yet 55, beard still brown) oversees from the high ground. No tremor yet—his hands steady at 1-2 Hz, normal human frequency.

He stands like forystusauður—the lead sheep others follow. Natural authority before it became diseased.

THE ABUNDANCE BEFORE
SHOT 1A: THE BLEEDING GIANT FEEDING GENERATIONS
EXT. BEACH - FLENSING SCENE - DAY

CLOSE ON the knife entering whale blubber. The cut is surgical—ten feet in a line so straight it follows invisible architecture.

Forty people work in choreographed efficiency. Each knows their role through generations of practice. Knives passed down until worn to nothing but atomic sharpness.

MAGNÚS makes the primary cuts—behind the pectoral fin, along the lateral line. Steam rises from exposed flesh, body temperature meeting June air.

Where the heart should be pure muscle: a BRASS HEART VALVE, industrial perfect, mechanically threaded. It still pumps, sending contaminated blood through organic veins.

JÓN (8, no fever yet) points at the brass.

JÓN
Pabbi, hvað er það?

Father, what is that?

MAGNÚS
(commanding)
Ekki horfa. Ekki sjá.

Don't look. Don't see.

But SIGRID looks. Sees. On the brass: "GRIMSBY STEEL WORKS 1884."

The whale was contaminated three years before death. Swimming through their waters as hybrid of nature and manufacture.

CREATIVE NOTES - SHOT 1A:

SHOT 1B: HÁKARL FERMENTATION AND BLOOD POOLING
EXT. BEACH - CONTINUOUS

The blood pools reveal their own horror—perfect circles, too perfect, as if something beneath the sand creates collection points. The blood doesn't soak in but pools at exactly 37°C, body temperature, staying warm without reason.

Near the tide line: hákarl fermentation pits. Poisonous shark meat buried to rot safely, controlled decay that takes poison and makes it food.

But this year's hákarl shows green spots where there should be white. Industrial contamination even in controlled rot.

An OLD WOMAN (GUÐRÚN's grandmother) whispers:

OLD GUÐRÚN
Blóðið veit. Það veit hvenær
eigin ending byrjar.

Blood knows. It knows when
its own ending begins.

THE FEAST
SHOT 4B: CHILDREN'S PROPHECY IN THE NEUTRAL FIELD
EXT. FIELD BETWEEN FARMS - LATE AFTERNOON

Five children from different farms meet at boundaries—where property lines blur and ownership becomes theoretical.

They play the knucklebone game, but every throw creates the same pattern: five points, six intersections.

Winter.

SHOT 5A: THE IMPOSSIBLE FEAST TABLE
EXT. FIELD ABOVE BEACH - SUNSET

Three tables arranged in horseshoe formation. Fifty people seated by invisible hierarchies—ancient grudges, marriage possibilities, blood relations creating complex positioning.

The whale meat steams on platters, dark red with iron. Dried fish pyramid in architecture. Berry wine purple-black in clay jugs.

MAGNÚS rises for the toast. Opens his mouth to speak leadership—

Nothing. His lips move. No sound.

He tries again. On the third attempt, sound emerges: the BLEATING of sheep. High, panicked.

Everyone laughs, thinking it's a joke. But his face shows terror. He doesn't know he's prophesying his transformation.

On the horizon: HMS GRIMSBY. A trawler that won't be built for three years, but its searchlight sweeps the coast in broad daylight. Yellow industrial light makes everyone's skin look already dead.

MAGNÚS
(finding his voice)
Hunsa það. Það er ekki til.

Ignore it. It doesn't exist.

But Sigrid knows: the trawler is more real than they are.

In the dream, they are happy.

DEEP ANALYSIS - PROLOGUE AS FOUNDATION:

The Shadow Pole as Witness Point:
The pole is where British sailors who haven't died yet placed a marker for ships that haven't wrecked yet. Its impossible shadow measures not light but time, always pointing toward transformation moments. Sigrid returns to it because it's the only point where all of Iceland's realities are visible simultaneously.

Industrial Contamination as Time Travel:
The whale's brass heart valve from 1884, the Grimsby Steel Works stamps, the HMS Grimsby appearing three years before it's built—industry extracts so thoroughly it reaches backward through time, contaminating the past with the future's hunger. The divine whale swims through Iceland's waters already infected with its ending.

Children as Prophets:
Children understand transformation first because they haven't learned to unknow impossible things. Their knucklebone games, their emerging sheep teeth, their unconscious réttir formations—they're already practicing being livestock while adults perform humanity's last gestures.

The Feast as Final Performance:
The abundance feast is everyone performing prosperity while knowing it's ending. Magnús bleating instead of speaking is his future voice erupting through his present throat. The synchronized chewing, the unconscious "mæ" sounds between swallows—they're rehearsing sheep behavior while pretending to be human.

Blood as Calendar:
The ram's blood drawing impossible mathematics doesn't just mark its death but creates the temporal architecture for transformation. Five spirals creating six points is the prologue creating space for empire to exist. The blood remembers every future, warns every past, but must be spilled anyway.

Mathematics of Extraction:
Counting three terrains but mouth forming four. Five people becoming six. Forty-three preserved fish becoming forty-one without theft. Reality itself is being edited, history extracted before it happens. The British trawlers don't just take fish—they take the possibility of fish ever having existed.

The Privilege of Witness:
Sigrid stands at exactly 11 feet distance throughout the prologue, maintaining witness space. She's marked from the beginning as the one who will escape through transformation into raven. The shadow pole chose her, the dying whale saw her, the 500 sheep recognized her as already raven. She will carry testimony.

Why This Prologue Matters:
Without seeing the abundance, we can't understand the loss. Without seeing Magnús at 45 with steady hands and natural authority, we can't understand his tremoring tyranny at 55. Without seeing the children with human teeth, their sheep teeth emerging are just folklore. The prologue IS the proof that transformation isn't metaphor but documentary.

The prologue contains every ending in its beginning, every beginning in its ending. The raven dying on the beach contains Iceland's entire summer of 1887 in its throat. When we emerge through its beak into winter 1888, we carry that summer with us—the last time Iceland was whole, before mathematics broke, before shadows gained sources, before abundance became extraction.

FINAL FRAME OF PROLOGUE:

The ram's blood in snow. Still steaming. Still impossible. Five spirals with six intersections.

The pattern that explains everything.
The wound that never heals.
The testimony that can't end.

FADE TO:

The cosmic abandonment of Shot 1, main story. The aurora borealis pulling away from Earth. The old gods saying goodbye. The transformation beginning.

Production Notes:

This prologue runs approximately 22-25 minutes, creating a complete first act that exists in summer before the winter transformation. It should be filmed with the same handheld, documentary approach as the main story, but with golden/green tones of false summer rather than the blue/white of winter death.

The shadow pole remains standing throughout the entire film—visible in background shots, its impossible shadow always pointing toward the current transformation moment. It's the only constant between prologue and main story.

The raven's throat interior should be filmed with medical endoscopy equipment, making Iceland's memory anatomically real. We are literally inside the dying memory of the nation.

All industrial contamination (brass fixtures, rope through flesh, manufacturing stamps) should be practically built into the whale and ram props. No CGI—the contamination must feel medically real, surgically present.

The children's sheep teeth should be subtle prosthetics, barely visible behind baby teeth. The transformation is already happening but adults refuse to see it.

The feast scene requires 50 background actors, all trained to unconsciously synchronize their chewing into ruminant rhythm. The "mæ" sounds between swallows should be barely audible, almost subliminal.

The ram's death run must be one continuous shot—12 minutes of the ram running its blood spiral while Magnús follows. No cuts. The mathematics must be drawn in real time.

The camera recognition moment where Iceland sees itself watching—this is the film's crucial turn. The audience realizes they ARE Iceland, watching their own transformation through their own dying eye.
